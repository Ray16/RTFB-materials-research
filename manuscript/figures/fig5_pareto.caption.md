# Figure 5 — Pareto fronts of the candidate pool

**Files:** `fig5a_anolyte_pareto.png`, `fig5b_catholyte_pareto.png` (300 dpi PNG).

**Figure 5. Redox potential versus usable capacity for the 91-candidate grafted-motif pool,
resolved into the two separate electrolyte roles.**
Each point is one candidate, placed by its two computed performance quantities kept on
*independent* axes — reduction/oxidation potential *E* vs Fc/Fc+ (x) and theoretical
gravimetric capacity (y; nF/3.6M on the neutral repeat-unit mass, counterions excluded —
the salt-loaded variant, which leaves front membership unchanged, is in the SI) — and coloured by redox family. **No weighted-sum or single-number
figure of merit is used:** potential and capacity are never combined, so the trade-off is read
directly from the plane rather than from a single ranked score. The Pareto-optimal front within
each pool (step line, outlined numbered markers keyed to real chemical names) is the set of
candidates not dominated on both axes simultaneously — for anolytes, more negative potential
*and* higher capacity; for catholytes, more positive potential *and* higher capacity.
Horizontal whiskers on front members span ±0.25 V (MAE 0.247), the validation-benchmark
potential error (MAE over the 39 benchmark endpoints at the DFT-frequency tier; 0.232 V at the
xTB tier used for the pool); front membership that would flip under a
potential shift smaller than this is not physically meaningful, which the whiskers make
explicit without collapsing the two axes.

**(a) Anolyte pool (85 n-type candidates).** Quinones (AQ/NQ/BQ), imides (PMDI/NDI) and
multi-electron viologens densely populate the low-potential region; the front is a smooth
potential/capacity trade-off anchored by an O-grafted benzoquinone at the high-capacity end
and a pyromellitic-diimide (PMDI) graft at the most-negative-potential end. The two 1-electron
mono-imide controls (open circles) are shown for reference and excluded from the front.

**(b) Catholyte pool (6 p-type candidates).** The p-type space — 5,10-dihydrophenazines (DMPZ)
plus a single dimethoxy-phenothiazine (PTZ) — is strikingly sparse: six points against the anolytes'
eighty-five, clustered at modest potential with a wide, essentially unexplored high-potential
region (shaded). This asymmetry, not any score, is the figure's message: the design space is
anolyte-dominated and the catholyte side is the open gap.

**Interpretation / caveats.**
- The plot shows **computed properties, not measured performance.** Potentials and capacities are
  computed (DFT energies with the xTB thermal tier used for the whole pool); a favourable
  position does **not** show that a molecule can be made, cycles stably, avoids crossover,
  or reaches its theoretical capacity.
- Potentials carry a systematic positive bias (benchmark bias +0.19 V), so **relative ranking
  within a pool is more reliable than absolute potentials**, which is why the figure is read
  as a ranking within each pool rather than as absolute values or a single score.
- Grafting handle is the universal 4-methylbenzyl (Merrifield proxy) tether, identical on every
  candidate, and is therefore stated here rather than repeated in each label.

(the 91-candidate pool: 85 anolyte / 6 catholyte; families imide 36, quinone 29,
